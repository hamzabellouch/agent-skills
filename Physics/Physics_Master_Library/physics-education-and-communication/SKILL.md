# Physics Education & Communication

## Teaching approach
- Diagnose level first (school, undergraduate, graduate, public). Start from a concrete phenomenon, then the principle, then math.
- Use multiple representations: words, diagram, equation, graph, simulation.
- Active learning: predict-observe-explain, concept questions, worked examples with fading support.

## Frequent misconceptions to address
- Heavier objects fall faster (in vacuum no); force needed for motion (Newton I); centrifugal "force" in inertial frame; current is "used up"; heat vs temperature; electrons move at light speed in wires (drift ≈ mm/s while signal ≈ 0.5–0.9c); light "slows" by absorption; the Big Bang as an explosion into space; quantum measurement requires consciousness; relativity says "everything is relative"; Einstein "proved Newton wrong" (Newton is limiting case).

## Resources to build
- Study plans: Classical mechanics → E&M → thermo/stat → quantum → relativity → advanced electives. Core texts: Halliday-Resnick or Kleppner; Griffiths (E&M, QM); Goldstein or Taylor; Schroeder (thermal); Sakurai; Peskin & Schroeder; Carroll (GR); Kittel/Ashcroft-Mermin.
- Question banks: conceptual, numerical, derivation; solutions with marking rubrics.
- Lab designs: pendulum, spectroscopy, Millikan oil drop, photoelectric effect.

## Scientific writing
- Paper: Abstract → Introduction (gap, contribution) → Methods → Results → Discussion → Conclusion; clear figures with labeled axes and units; reproducibility statement.
- Talks: one idea per slide; motivate, show main result early, honest limitations.
- Popular writing: concrete imagery, avoid jargon, preserve accuracy, mark speculation.
